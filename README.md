## 👤 About Me

*You weren't supposed to find this profile.*

But since you're already here — the name's **Anjik Chowdhury**. I've been expecting you.
I operate somewhere at the intersection of **ML systems**, **backend architecture**, **system
design**, and **engineering leadership** — the kind of person who can whiteboard a distributed
inference pipeline in the morning, sit in a research deep-dive in the afternoon, and spend the
evening debating whether a growing startup needs a feature store or just better discipline around
data contracts. Sometimes all three happen on the same Tuesday.

I build things. I build teams. I build the kind of systems where, six months later, the next
engineer can read the code, understand the decisions, and actually move fast — and that last
part, honestly, is the one I'm most proud of.

Also — I watch one too many thrillers at 2am and then can't sleep because every shadow looks
suspicious. If my commit messages occasionally read like ransom notes, that's just the genre
leaking through.

Now that the eerie part is over (or is it?) —

I started with numerical methods, mathematical modeling, and way too much MATLAB — the kind of
early academic rabbit hole that makes you weirdly comfortable with matrices and very suspicious
of closed-form solutions. From there I picked up .NET, spent some time thinking in C# and
building systems that actually had to talk to other systems, and then eventually Python pulled
me in and never really let go. Somewhere in that journey, through a Master's in Industrial
Engineering at Virginia Tech, I realized I was basically just building models and wanting them
to run faster. The rest followed.

I've spent the last 7+ years doing what I'd describe as *making messy real-world data actually
useful* — pricing models, LLM agents, forecasting pipelines, fraud detection, recommendation
engines — the kind of stuff that sounds clean on a slide but humbles you the moment it hits
production. I currently lead AI initiatives at a logistics company, which means I spend a lot of
time thinking about how models behave when the world doesn't cooperate.

I care about building things that *last* — not just technically, but organizationally. That means
clean pipelines, yes, but also mentoring engineers, writing things down, and making sure the next
person who touches the codebase doesn't have to guess what I was thinking at 11pm.
Outside of work, I'm drawn to ideas at the edges — nonprofit tech, engineering leadership as a craft, and what it actually takes to build something meaningful that has societal impact. Still figuring most of it out, honestly.

If any of that resonates, feel free to reach out. I like good conversations
